Get Atelier

Offer Atelier to your customers

For signing services. Sign Atelier with each customer's certificate and put that certificate inside it. Atelier then opens ready to install their apps, and keeps itself up to date.

What your customers get

  • Atelier, ready to use. It opens with their certificate already in it, so they can install apps straight away.
  • Updates without you. Atelier signs and installs its own new releases with that certificate, and keeps its data. There's nothing for you to host.
  • Your apps, if you have a source. They can add it in Atelier, then install and update its apps there.

You'll need

  • Atelier's unsigned IPA, from its releases or its source.
  • Each customer's certificate: the .p12, its password and the .mobileprovision that lists their iPhone, the same files you give them.
  • A signer that signs app extensions, such as zsign.
Rather not sign it yourselves?

Send customers to Install Atelier with a signing service. Safari signs Atelier on their iPhone with the certificate they download from you, and puts it inside.

    Put the certificate inside

    Before you sign, add AtelierCertificate.plist next to Atelier's Info.plist, so the signature seals it with the rest. On its first launch, Atelier checks that the profile it was installed with lists the certificate, and imports it.

    p12data
    The .p12.
    profiledata
    Its .mobileprovision.
    passwordstring
    The .p12's password.
    idstring
    A new UUID each time you sign, so Atelier imports the certificate again when it's installed again.
    namestring, optional
    What Atelier calls the certificate, such as your service's name. Without it, the certificate's own name.
    udidstring, optional
    The customer's UDID. Atelier then checks that profiles list their iPhone, without asking them to set anything up.

    An XML or binary property list. Releases that look for it have AtelierTakesCertificate set to true in their Info.plist.

    The IPA then holds the certificate

    Anyone with it has the .p12 and its password. Serve each customer's Atelier only to them, at an address only they get, and delete it once iOS has downloaded it.

    Without it, customers import the certificate in Atelier's Get Ready, from the files you give them.

  3. 3

    Sign it with the same certificate

    Use the customer's certificate and profile, the ones you put inside. Atelier signs its own updates with that certificate, and iOS only installs an update over Atelier when it's signed for the same App ID.

    Keep the bundle ID, com.atelier.app

    iOS treats another bundle ID as another app: a second Atelier, without the first one's sources, certificates and apps.

    • Sign with the profile's entitlements as they are, as zsign and Feather do. Atelier asks for none of its own.
    • Sign its extension, PlugIns/AtelierLiveActivity.appex, with the same profile, before the app, rather than removing it. It shows the pairing code during the setup for installs without iOS's alert.
    • Development, ad hoc and enterprise profiles all work, for one App ID or a wildcard. App Store profiles don't.
  4. 4

    Install it

    Install it as you install your other apps. With a development or ad hoc certificate, iOS opens Atelier once Developer Mode is on, in Settings › Privacy & Security, which restarts the iPhone. With an enterprise one, it opens once the customer trusts it in Settings › General › VPN & Device Management. Atelier then opens to Get Ready, with the certificate done.

Good to know

Once it's installed

Something missing, or not working for your service? Open an issue on GitHub.

Who keeps Atelier up to date?

Atelier does. Its own source is built in and can't be removed, and Atelier checks it for its updates. It downloads each new release, signs it with the certificate it was installed with, keeping its bundle ID and App ID, and installs it over itself, keeping its data. Customers who have Atelier don't need a new one from you for each release, and there's no need to list it in your source.

Can customers add our source?

Yes. Atelier reads AltStore sources, and GitHub repos too. Link customers to https://atelierstore.app/source?url= followed by your source's address, percent-encoded. It opens Atelier at your source, ready to add. From your own app, atelier://source?url= does the same.

What happens when Apple revokes a certificate?

Atelier stops opening, like every app signed with it. Sign Atelier again with the customer's new certificate, with that one inside, and offer it to them again. It installs over the old Atelier, keeps its sources and apps, and imports the new certificate, and Re-sign All then signs their other apps with it.

That only works when the new profile is for the same App ID, from the same team. iOS won't install Atelier over one signed for another App ID, so the customer would have to delete Atelier first, and lose its sources, certificates and list of apps.

What if a customer already has Atelier?

If theirs is signed for the same App ID as yours, yours installs over it and keeps its data. If not, as when they installed it with another certificate, iOS won't install yours until they delete theirs. They can keep theirs instead and import your certificate in it: it updates itself either way.

What if we change the bundle ID?

Atelier still works, and updates itself under the bundle ID it was installed with. But each bundle ID is another app to iOS, so a customer who installs Atelier again under another one gets a second Atelier, without the first one's sources, certificates and apps.

The extension's bundle ID has to start with the app's, as <bundle ID>.LiveActivity. And don't end a bundle ID in .atelier2, .atelier3 and so on: Atelier reads those as copies of itself.

May we offer Atelier?

Yes. Atelier is free software under the GNU General Public License v3.0, so you can offer it signed, as long as you say where its source is: link to its GitHub repo. Atelier doesn't endorse or recommend any signing service, including the ones that offer it.
