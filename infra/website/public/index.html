How it works

Tap Install. Atelier does the rest.

Downloading, signing and installing an app that isn't on the App Store, known as sideloading, usually means many steps that are often complicated, unpredictable and hard to follow unless you're technical. In Atelier it's one button, the same experience you're used to from the App Store, just as it should be.

  1. Your appDownloading · 6.1 of 9.7 MB

    1Download

    Atelier downloads the app from its source, and picks up where it left off if the connection drops.

  2. Your appSigning with Development Certificate

    2Sign

    It signs the app so iOS will run it, with any of the signing methods iOS supports, right on your iPhone. No jailbreak needed.

  3. Your appInstalled

    3Install

    iOS installs it, and Atelier checks that it's there, then keeps it up to date without losing its data. Atelier keeps itself up to date too.

Features

Every way to sign and sideload, in one place.

That's the goal, and it's a work in progress. Today Atelier signs with your own certificate or your Apple ID, free or paid, and installs with or without iOS's alert.

Your sources, together

Add AltStore sources and GitHub repos. Browse their apps and news in one place, with pre-releases if you want them.

Your certificate or Apple ID

Import a certificate from a signing service, or sign in with a free or paid Apple ID, as AltStore and SideStore do. Atelier supports multiple Apple IDs simultaneously, so free ones can add more new apps a week. With an Apple ID, widgets keep showing their app's data too.

Updates, ready ahead

Atelier checks your sources in the background and downloads updates on Wi‑Fi, so Update only has to sign and install.

Re-sign everything

Atelier checks with Apple that your certificate is still valid. When it expires or Apple revokes it, Re-sign All signs your apps again with one that works, and installs them over the old ones.

Installs in the background

A one-time setup lets Atelier install apps and updates without iOS's alert, even after you leave it. It needs Wi‑Fi and the LocalDevVPN app.

Open source

Atelier is free under the GPL-3.0. Signing happens on your iPhone, and your certificate isn't uploaded anywhere.

And: install a second copy of an app with its own data, go back to an older version, see every install in Install History, and open AltStore, SideStore and Feather links and files. Atelier updates itself, too.

FAQ

Questions

Is Atelier on the App Store?

No, and it can't be: the App Store doesn't allow apps that install other apps. You install Atelier once yourself, and then it updates itself from its own source.

What certificate do I need?

None, if you sign in with a free Apple ID, as AltStore and SideStore do: Atelier makes one with it. But the apps it signs only open for 7 days after they're signed, so Atelier signs them again every week, on Wi‑Fi, to keep them working.

A free Apple ID also has two limits that are easy to mix up. One is how many apps you keep: your iPhone holds 3 apps signed with free Apple IDs at a time, and a fourth needs one of them deleted first. The other is how many new apps you add: each free Apple ID can install 10 new apps a week, and only an app's first install counts, not its updates. Atelier supports multiple Apple IDs simultaneously, so each one you add gives 10 more new apps a week, but your iPhone still holds 3 at a time.

A paid Apple ID means joining Apple's Developer Program, for $99 a year. The apps it signs open for a year, without those limits, and its certificate is yours alone, so it's very unlikely to be revoked.

A signing service usually costs a fraction of that, for a year too. It registers your iPhone on its own developer account and shares that account's certificate with many customers, so Apple can revoke it at any time. When Apple does, the apps signed with it stop opening until you get a new one.

Atelier doesn't endorse or suggest any signing service, so you'll need to look around: search for “iOS sideloading certificate” or “UDID registration”, and read what their customers say. Make sure the one you choose lets you download your certificate. That's what Atelier needs to work in the first place: this website installs Atelier with it, and Atelier signs your apps with it. Ideally, the service also replaces your certificate if Apple revokes it.

Do widgets and extensions work?

With an Apple ID, yes. An app shares its data with its widgets and extensions, like its share sheet or keyboard, through what Apple calls App Groups. Signed with your Apple ID, those groups get new names, and most apps only look under the old ones, so their widgets would come up empty. Atelier adds the groups to your Apple ID and puts a small library in the app that points the old names to the new ones, so the widget shows what the app has. If an app doesn't open with it, turn off Share App Groups in Custom Install › Advanced.

With a signing service's certificate, widgets and extensions still open, but usually can't see the app's data: the service decides which App Groups there are.

Do I need a computer?

No. You can install Atelier straight from this website with a signing service's certificate, or with Feather, AltStore or SideStore on your iPhone. You only need a computer for the first install if you use Sideloadly. After that, everything happens on the iPhone.

Why does iOS ask before each install?

Out of the box, each install goes through iOS's own alert. The one-time setup in Settings › Installs lets Atelier install directly instead, without the alert and in the background. It needs Wi‑Fi and the LocalDevVPN app.

What happens when my certificate expires or is revoked?

The apps signed with it stop opening. Atelier checks with Apple whether your certificate was revoked, lists the apps that need signing again, and Re-sign All signs them with a certificate that works.

How is it different from AltStore, SideStore and Feather?

Atelier brings their best parts together: sources, news and updates like AltStore and SideStore, and signing with your own certificate on the iPhone like Feather, all behind one Install button. Unlike them, it can also add GitHub repos as sources, so it doesn't depend on developers publishing an AltStore source, and it keeps multiple Apple IDs signed in simultaneously. It aims to be as easy to use as possible. It works out of the box, asks for as little setup as it can, and keeps that setup simple. It also keeps itself updated.

Is it safe?

Atelier is open source, so anyone can read what it does. It signs on your iPhone, and your certificate isn't uploaded anywhere. When you install Atelier from this website, your browser signs it too, on your iPhone or computer. With Include Certificate on, your certificate goes inside Atelier, and this site keeps the signed Atelier at a private address until iOS downloads it, for 10 minutes at most, and logs nothing. Add only sources you trust, since Atelier installs what they list.

Does it work on iPad?

It runs on iPad with iPadOS 17 or later, but it has only been tested on iPhone so far.
